Keep your carts. Keep their batteries. Hyperion's smart bidirectional chargers connect every cart in your fleet into a single energy storage system that charges when electricity is cheap and powers the course when electricity is expensive.

Not by buying batteries. By using the ones already parked in your cart barn.


No new batteries

Your fleet's existing packs are the storage. Nothing to buy, nothing to site, no container of batteries behind the maintenance building.

No change to operations

Carts plug in at the end of the round the same way they do today. The chargers handle the rest.

Carts charged by first tee

Play comes first. The system always keeps enough charge in the fleet for tomorrow's rounds, and only uses the surplus.

How it works


  1. Swap the chargers, not the carts. Hyperion bidirectional chargers replace the chargers in your cart barn. Your carts and their stock batteries stay exactly as they are.
  2. Charge when power is cheap. Overnight and midday, when your utility's rates are lowest, the system charges the whole fleet.
  3. Discharge when power is expensive. During the late-afternoon peak, the chargers run in reverse and feed energy from the cart batteries back into the clubhouse, pump house, and maintenance shop — and never draw from the grid during those hours.
  4. Every cart, one system. Hyperion's software coordinates all the chargers so 70–80 carts act as a single energy storage system sized in the hundreds of kilowatt-hours.

Where the savings come from


Energy arbitrage

Power your course uses during expensive peak hours is bought overnight at the off-peak rate instead. Same kilowatt-hours, cheaper price.

Peak shaving and demand charges

Commercial bills are driven by your single highest demand spike, not just total usage. Discharging the fleet into those spikes — and never charging carts during them — pulls your peak down and takes the demand charge with it.

Cart charging moved off-peak

Charging a fleet of carts is a large load. Today it often lands in peak hours. Hyperion shifts all of it to the cheapest hours of the day.

More value from your solar

Already have panels, or considering them? Store the midday surplus in the cart batteries instead of exporting it for a low credit, then use it after the sun goes down.

Backup power for the course

When the grid goes down, the fleet keeps the clubhouse, POS, irrigation, and refrigeration running so play and food service continue.

No storage capital

Standalone commercial battery systems cost six figures. You already bought your batteries — this is the equipment that finally puts them to work.
